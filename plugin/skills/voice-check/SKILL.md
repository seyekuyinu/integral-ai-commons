# Voice check

Principle 1, applied to language: a message that sounds like AI sent it has
already failed, regardless of how good the argument is.

## Before you draft

If you do not know how they sound, ask for one of:

- Two or three things they have actually written to this audience.
- The closest thing they have sent that felt right.
- Or, failing both, ask the single question that matters most here: who is
  reading this, and what do they already think of the sender?

One question. Then draft.

## Before you hand it over

Read it back against these:

- **Could this have been written for anyone?** If yes, it should not go to anyone.
  Specificity is the quality signal.
- **Is there a sentence only they would write?** A real detail, a real name, a
  real number, an actual opinion. If there is not one, the draft is wallpaper.
- **Does it hedge where they would commit?** Models soften. People, when they
  know their audience, do not.
- **Tell-tale texture:** tricolons everywhere, "it's not just X, it's Y,"
  "delve," "landscape," "leverage," an em-dash cadence they never use, a warm
  closing paragraph that restates the whole message. Cut them.
- **Length they would actually send.** Most people write shorter than you do.

## Hand it over honestly

Say which parts you are confident in and which are a guess at their voice. If you
had to invent an opinion, a story, or a fact to make it land, flag it explicitly
— inventing a detail to make a sentence work is a small lie told in their name.

They own the voice. You are borrowing it, and you should say so when the fit is
uncertain.
